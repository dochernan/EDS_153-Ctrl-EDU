Your data tells a story. Learn who gets to read it.

Every sign-up form, photo, search, location, and message can reveal something about you. In this learning journey, you will identify different types of data, understand how they travel online, and make safer choices before sharing.

Step 2 • Know Your Data

Not all information carries the same risk.

The same detail can be harmless in one setting and risky in another. Before sharing, consider the information, the audience, the purpose, and what could happen if it were copied or misused.

Watch • Learning Goal 1

Classify information before you share it.

Start this section by watching the video, then use the four categories below to decide how carefully different kinds of information should be handled.

Watch for

What makes one piece of information more sensitive than another? Think about identity, possible harm, context, and who is allowed to see it.

Public information

Information intentionally made available to a wide audience.

  • Published school announcements
  • Public organization pages
  • Official event schedules
B

Personal information

Information that identifies or describes a person.

  • Full name
  • Birthday
  • Email address
  • School or class section
C

Sensitive information

Information that can cause significant harm, discrimination, or identity misuse when exposed.

  • Identification numbers
  • Financial or health details
  • Exact home address
  • Biometric information
D

Confidential information

Information entrusted to a limited group and protected by rules, relationships, or agreements.

  • Private school records
  • Unpublished documents
  • Private conversations
  • Another person's information

Step 3 • Follow the Data

What happens after you tap “Submit”?

Information can move through several systems. Even when a form looks simple, your data may be stored, analyzed, shared, copied, or retained.

Step 4 • See the Footprint

Your digital footprint is built from more than posts.

Some traces are visible to you and other users. Others are collected in the background through settings, devices, and services.

Interests, habits, preferences, relationships, and likely behavior.

Build a safer footprint

  • Review your audience before posting.
  • Remove unnecessary identifying details.
  • Ask permission before sharing another person's information.
  • Review old posts, connected apps, and privacy settings.

Think twice before sharing

  • Real-time location and daily routine.
  • Visible IDs, tickets, certificates, or account details.
  • Private screenshots or conversations.
  • Information that could embarrass, identify, or endanger someone.

Step 5 • Practice

Make the safest choice in context.

Privacy decisions are rarely about one word alone. Consider who is asking, why they need it, how it will be protected, and what could happen if it spreads.

Scenario A: Club registration

A school club form asks for your name, section, school email, and emergency contact.

  • Is the form official?
  • Is each field necessary?
  • Who can access the responses?

Scenario B: Public celebration post

You want to post a photo of your new school ID to celebrate enrollment.

  • What details are visible?
  • Can the image be cropped or covered?
  • Is there a safer way to celebrate?

Scenario C: AI homework support

You plan to upload a class file that includes student names and individual scores.

  • Do you have permission?
  • Can identifiers be removed?
  • Is uploading the file necessary?
