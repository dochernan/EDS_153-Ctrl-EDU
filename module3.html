Quick reflection

What usually makes a message feel trustworthy?

There may be several answers
Familiarity

A known name or logo

Attackers can copy names, profile photos, signatures, and branding. Familiar appearance is not proof.

Emotion

Urgency or fear

Pressure reduces careful thinking. A legitimate message should still survive independent verification.

Reward

A prize or opportunity

Unexpected rewards can be used to make risky requests feel exciting or harmless.

Step 2 • Read the Signals

One clue may be harmless. Several clues form a pattern.

Instead of asking only, “Does this look real?” ask, “What evidence supports or weakens this message?”

S

Sender

Check the full address, account history, display name, spelling, and whether the contact method is expected.

P

Pressure

Be cautious when a message creates panic, secrecy, limited time, or fear of punishment.

R

Request

Question requests for passwords, codes, payments, personal details, downloads, or account changes.

L

Link or QR code

Inspect the destination before opening it. A short link, QR code, or familiar text can hide a different site.

A

Attachment

Unexpected files may contain malicious content or lead to fake sign-in pages.

C

Context

Ask whether the message makes sense based on your recent activity, school process, or relationship with the sender.

1. PauseDo not act while pressured or distracted.
2. InspectCheck the sender, request, destination, and context.
3. VerifyUse a trusted website, known number, or separate contact method.
4. ReportFlag the message and ask for help when appropriate.

Step 3 • Message Detective

Find the warning signs.

This fictional message contains several clues. Select each clue to reveal why it deserves attention.

From: Student Support <support-office@school-helpdesk-login.net> Subject: URGENT: Your account will be closed today
Dear Student,

We detected an error in your account. Confirm your password and verification code within 15 minutes or your school files will be permanently deleted. Do not contact your teacher because this is an automated security process. https://school-account-check.example/verify

0 of 5 clues reviewed

Impersonation

“Your teacher needs gift cards now.”

Verify using a known number or in person. A familiar name does not prove who controls the account.

Code theft

“Send me the code you just received.”

Never share one-time passwords or approval codes. An unexpected code may mean someone is attempting to sign in.

QR phishing

“Scan to claim your school reward.”

A QR code hides its destination until scanned. Verify the source and inspect the address before proceeding.

Step 4 • Inspect Links and Files

Look beyond what the message displays.

A link can use familiar words while leading somewhere else. A file can look ordinary while creating unnecessary risk.

Safer inspection habits

  • Use the official app or type the known website address yourself.
  • Check the complete domain carefully, including spelling and endings.
  • Verify unexpected files with the sender through another channel.
  • Preview QR-code destinations before continuing, when your device allows it.
  • Ask a trusted adult or school contact when uncertain.

Risky shortcuts

  • Trusting a link because the visible text looks official.
  • Opening a file because it was sent by a familiar display name.
  • Entering credentials after following an unexpected message.
  • Scanning unknown QR codes without checking the destination.
  • Ignoring browser or device warnings.

Step 5 • Respond Safely

Do not only avoid the trap. Know what to do next.

A calm response protects you and may help protect other people from the same attempt.

Do not interactAvoid clicking, replying, scanning, downloading, or sharing.
Verify separatelyContact the person or organization using a known, trusted channel.
ReportUse the platform's report feature or inform the appropriate school contact.
Secure your accountIf you interacted, change affected credentials, review sessions, and enable MFA.
