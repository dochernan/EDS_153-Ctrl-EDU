Module 1

Your Data, Your Identity

Learn how personal information becomes part of your digital identity and why the same detail can be harmless in one situation but sensitive in another.

Personal data Sensitive information Consent Digital footprints

What You Will Learn

  • Classify information as public, personal, sensitive, or confidential.
  • Explain how websites and applications collect and use data.
  • Recognize how posts, forms, photos, and accounts build a digital footprint.
  • Make more informed choices before sharing information.

What You Will Do

  • Complete a “Safe to Share?” classification challenge.
  • Trace how one piece of information can travel online.
  • Review a fictional sign-up form for unnecessary data requests.
  • Create a personal data-awareness checklist.

02

Module 2

Lock It Down

Build stronger defenses around the accounts and devices that store your messages, files, schoolwork, photographs, and identity information.

Passphrases Password reuse Multi-factor authentication Account recovery

Stronger Protection

  • Use a unique passphrase for every important account.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication where available.
  • Keep recovery details accurate and secure.
  • Lock devices and install trusted updates.

Common Weaknesses

  • Reusing one password across many accounts.
  • Sharing passwords with friends or classmates.
  • Using easily guessed personal details.
  • Ignoring suspicious sign-in alerts.

Module 3

Spot the Trap

Learn how attackers use urgency, fear, rewards, authority, and familiar-looking messages to persuade people to click, reply, pay, or reveal information.

Phishing Impersonation Suspicious links Social engineering

Pause and Inspect

  • Check the sender, address, wording, and destination of links.
  • Verify urgent requests through a separate trusted channel.
  • Avoid opening unexpected attachments.
  • Report suspicious messages instead of forwarding them.

Warning Signs

  • Threats that demand immediate action.
  • Unexpected rewards, prizes, or refunds.
  • Requests for passwords, codes, or payment.
  • Links or sender details that are almost—but not exactly—correct.

04

Module 4

Share with Care

Take greater control of what applications, websites, classmates, and strangers can see, collect, copy, or redistribute.

Privacy settings App permissions Location sharing Cloud files

Share More Safely

  • Review who can see a post, file, image, or location.
  • Give applications only the permissions they genuinely need.
  • Remove hidden personal details before posting or uploading.
  • Ask permission before sharing another person's data or image.

Think Twice Before Sharing

  • Real-time locations and daily routines.
  • Identification cards, tickets, and documents with visible details.
  • Public cloud links containing private schoolwork or records.
  • Images or screenshots involving other people without consent.

Module 5

Privacy in the Age of AI

AI tools can be useful, but prompts, images, files, and chat histories may contain information that should not be exposed or reused.

Safe prompting Anonymization File uploads Third-party data

Protect Data Before Using AI

  • Replace real names and identifiers with fictional placeholders.
  • Remove confidential details from documents and screenshots.
  • Use general descriptions instead of private personal information.
  • Follow school rules and platform privacy guidance.

Do Not Upload

  • Passwords, account-recovery information, or authentication codes.
  • Private student records, identification documents, or medical details.
  • Confidential school or workplace files.
  • Personal information belonging to another person without permission.

Module 6

Act Fast, Recover Smart

A privacy or security incident can happen even when you are careful. The safest response is calm, timely, and supported by trusted people.

Account recovery Evidence preservation Reporting Personal action plan

KALASAG Response Steps

Secure first. Report next. Recover carefully.

Use these steps after a suspicious login, phishing attempt, exposed file, or compromised account.

  1. Stop the activity and disconnect when necessary.
  2. Secure the account and change affected credentials.
  3. Preserve screenshots, messages, and other evidence.
  4. Report the incident to a trusted adult or approved school contact.
  5. Review connected accounts, devices, and recovery settings.
  6. Monitor for continued suspicious activity.

Final KALASAG Challenge

Create your personal digital-protection plan.

Identify three security habits you will improve immediately, list the people or services you can contact for help, and schedule a regular privacy check for your important accounts.
