Module 6 • Act Fast, Recover Smart

When something goes wrong, your next steps matter.

A suspicious login, leaked password, lost device, accidental upload, or compromised account can feel overwhelming. This module will help you respond calmly, limit the damage, recover safely, and know when to ask for help.

Step 2 • Recognize the Warning Signs

Not every problem looks dramatic.

A security incident may begin with a small clue: a password-reset email you did not request, a login from an unfamiliar place, a message you did not send, changed account settings, missing files, or a device that suddenly behaves differently.

A

Account warning signs

Unexpected login alerts, changed passwords, unfamiliar active sessions, unknown recovery details, or messages sent from your account.

B

Data exposure signs

A private file becomes public, a screenshot is forwarded, a document is shared with the wrong person, or sensitive data is uploaded to an external service.

C

Device warning signs

A device is lost, stolen, unlocked, remotely accessed, or shows suspicious applications, pop-ups, or changes.

Step 3 • Immediate Response

Pause. Protect. Preserve. Report. Review.

A good response is calm and organized. Avoid deleting everything, confronting a suspected attacker, forwarding harmful material, or making random changes without understanding the situation.

1

Pause

Stop interacting with suspicious messages, links, files, or accounts.

2

Protect

Secure the account or device and stop further access or sharing.

3

Preserve

Record useful details such as time, sender, device, screenshot, or account alert.

4

Report

Contact the right person or service, especially when other people's data is involved.

5

Review

Check related accounts, privacy settings, devices, and recovery options.

Important: Never enter a password, recovery code, or verification code into this learning website. Practice activities should use fictional examples only.

Step 4 • Recover Control

Recovery is more than changing one password.

After securing the affected account or device, review how the incident may have affected connected services, saved sessions, recovery methods, files, and other people.

Incident Immediate action Recovery action
Compromised account Use the official recovery page, change the password, and end unfamiliar sessions. Enable MFA, review recovery details, check sent messages, and secure accounts that reused the password.
Lost or stolen device Use official device-location or remote-lock features when available and report the loss. Sign out remotely, change important passwords, review saved accounts, and replace access credentials if necessary.
Private file shared publicly Revoke the public link or permissions immediately. Review access logs, notify affected people, and replace or remove exposed information when possible.
Verification code shared Secure the account immediately and reject unexpected login attempts. Change the password, review sessions, check recovery options, and contact the service if access was lost.
Suspicious application installed Disconnect when necessary and stop entering credentials. Remove the application using trusted procedures, update the device, run approved security checks, and seek technical help.

Step 5 • Report and Reflect

Asking for help is part of a strong response.

Reporting is especially important when a school account, another person's information, threats, harassment, financial loss, or repeated unauthorized access is involved.

Report when

  • A school account or school-managed device is involved.
  • Another person's data may have been exposed.
  • You cannot regain access to an important account.
  • There are threats, impersonation, harassment, or blackmail.
  • Money, identity documents, or sensitive records are involved.
  • The incident continues after basic recovery actions.

Avoid these reactions

  • Hiding the incident because of embarrassment.
  • Forwarding suspicious content to friends for opinions.
  • Publicly accusing someone without verified evidence.
  • Paying, complying, or sharing more information under pressure.
  • Deleting all evidence before asking for help.
  • Using unverified “recovery” services or strangers online.
